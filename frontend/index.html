How this is calculated

Banks typically cap your total EMI obligations (existing + new) at a fixed percentage of your monthly income — called FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio), usually 40–60% depending on income level and lender. Max new EMI = (Income × FOIR%) − Existing EMIs. The loan amount is then back-calculated from that EMI using your chosen rate and tenure. This is an estimate — actual eligibility also depends on credit score, employer, property value and the specific lender's policy.

An important caveat

This isn't a fair apples-to-apples comparison, and it's worth reading before acting on it. Prepayment is a guaranteed, risk-free return equal to your loan's interest rate. A SIP's return depends on actual market performance and is never guaranteed — the corpus above assumes your expected return happens smoothly every single year, which real markets don't do. Treat the SIP number as a plausible estimate under that assumption, not a promise. Many people split the difference — investing some and prepaying some.

Frequently asked questions

How is home loan EMI calculated?

EMI is calculated using the formula P × r × (1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1), where P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the number of monthly installments (tenure in years × 12).

Is there a penalty for prepaying a home loan in India?

As per RBI rules, floating-rate loans to individual borrowers (such as most home loans) carry no prepayment or foreclosure penalty. Fixed-rate loans may carry a prepayment charge of up to 2%.

What tax benefits are available on a home loan?

Principal repayment qualifies for deduction under Section 80C (up to ₹1.5 lakh/year), and interest paid qualifies for deduction under Section 24(b) (up to ₹2 lakh/year for a self-occupied property).
